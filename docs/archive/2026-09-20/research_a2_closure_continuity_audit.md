# Research A2-Closure — continuity failure audit and engineering handoff

## Question and frozen evidence

A2-Final selected **2,572 TRAIN employed-weekday diaries + R1** at the independent decision layer: 77/80 requests succeeded, three timed out, none had invalid output. Its separate 90-minute micro-rollout had 5/16 complete, 3 TIMEOUT, 7 MAX_DECISIONS and one recording-harness architecture error. This closure asks why the seven capped episodes stopped; it does not rerun any historical cell, redraw the 2,572/643 TRAIN/EVAL split, tune Stage A thresholds, or infer unobserved behavior after a truncation.

The original A2-Final directory `run/evaluation/a2_final/real_20260917T101056138346Z/` was read-only throughout this stage. A new checksum manifest and all new analysis live under `run/evaluation/a2_closure/audit_20260917T111536910509Z/`. The seven capped cells were selected programmatically from `episode_rows.jsonl`, joined to their recorded trajectories and daily tick files, and audited entirely offline: **zero provider or other LLM requests, zero network requests**. The new output contains no raw prompt, hidden reasoning or credential.

## Deterministic classification method

Each capped episode has four recorded decisions and 60 observed simulated minutes. The fixed taxonomy is A repeated rejected action, B immediate-action churn, C same-state redecision, D trigger/duration mechanics, E prior–world mismatch, F need/obligation loop, G model-action oscillation, H mixed/no dominant cause, and I insufficient data. A–G receive evidence scores from recorded counts, not a model judge. A, B, C, D, F and G divide by four decisions; E divides prior-followed rejected decisions by prior-followed decisions (zero if none). Coarse state for C uses location, activity, money, inventory, hunger/energy rounded to 0.1, and work-due. A requires adjacent identical action/target/reason rejections without a coarse-state change. B requires an accepted immediate MOVE/BUY/EAT followed by another decision within one 15-minute tick while no timed activity is active. D counts recorded activity-completion or obligation-boundary triggers; F counts rejected proposals under a CRITICAL_NEED trigger; G requires a location A→B→A cycle. Missing mandatory fields produce I. The top score wins, except a top-two gap <0.10 yields H. One primary appearing in at least four of seven cells is a *global engineering dominant cause*, not a statistical result. Exact definitions and each cell's scores are in `scoring_method.json` and `max_decision_audit.json`.

## Seven-cell result

All 7/7 trajectories had the needed action, rule, state, trigger, prior, and tick records. Primary counts: **B=5, F=1, H=1; A=C=D=E=G=I=0**. Thus `GLOBAL_DOMINANT_CAUSE=B_IMMEDIATE_ACTION_CHURN` (5/7). The compact traces make the mechanism visible:

- Midday cells **5 CONTROL, 6 CANDIDATE, 13 CANDIDATE** each used two ordinary next-tick redecisions after immediate actions; a meal acquisition sequence or failed EAT consumed the four-decision budget before minute 90. Cell 14 CONTROL had three rejected need-driven proposals and was classified F, not a prior effect.
- Evening cells **7 and 16 CANDIDATE** each did `MOVE restaurant → BUY meal → EAT meal`, then proposed LEISURE at the restaurant and were rejected. They had three immediate-action followups and no sustained active minutes before the cap.
- Morning cell **9 CANDIDATE** combined an immediate MOVE, a WORK proposal outside its allowed window, a rejected EAT without inventory, and another MOVE. Its leading scores were too close for one primary, so it is H.

Every capped cell had four decisions by minute 60 and **zero recorded timed-activity minutes**. None contains two decision calls at the same simulation time. All triggers were recorded as `NO_ACTIVE_ACTIVITY`, `CRITICAL_NEED` or `ACTION_REJECTED`; there was no `ACTIVITY_COMPLETED`/boundary-trigger concentration. The dominant B pattern is therefore next-*tick* activity-free continuation after actions whose immediate semantics were intentionally frozen in A2.0, **not** evidence of a same-tick trigger bookkeeping bug. The four-decision cap is also tight for an ordinary MOVE→BUY→EAT chain plus one rejected proposal. It is not a 90-minute behavioral or idle estimate.

## Prior-specific audit

Across the seven cells, the prior appeared in 20 decision contexts; the model followed it 8 times (5 rule-executable, **3 rule-rejected**) and did not follow it 12 times. Cells **7, 9 and 16** each contain one prior-followed rejection among four decisions, meeting the prespecified 25% per-cell and three-cell thresholds: `PRIOR_LOOP_SIGNAL=YES`. In 7/16, the R1 hint `LEISURE` was infeasible at the restaurant; in 9, `WORK` was outside the work window. Cell 6 displayed a `WORK` hint at the restaurant, but the model **ignored** it when proposing an invalid EAT; it is not counted as a prior-followed rejection. This distinction matters: the signal documents a secondary feasibility weakness, not proof that retrieval caused the five-cell dominant immediate-action pattern. No cell has E as its primary classifier.

## Historical recording error and regression

A2-Final Stage B cell 1 used `scenario_name=a2_final_micro`. Its recorder therefore took the generic immediate-step validation path; `TrajectoryRecorder.finish_episode → validate_trajectory` rejected a final state that legitimately advanced during daily ticks. The historical exception was `ValueError: episode final state does not match last step`. That cell has no validated trajectory; request count and behavior fields are unknown, not zero. It remains invalid and **was not rerun or imputed**. Stage A used a separate fixed-state path and was unaffected. The other 15 Stage B cells used the corrected existing `scenario_name=neutral_day` path and produced valid daily trajectories. A new no-provider regression test reproduces a validator failure with the old name and passes the same morning scenario with `neutral_day`. No WorldState, rule, reducer, decision, provider or AgentSociety upstream semantics were changed.

## Runtime change gate and current answer

The required `NO_RUNTIME_CHANGE_NEEDED` gate does **not** apply: the audit found both a 5/7 global B cause and `PRIOR_LOOP_SIGNAL=YES`. Nonetheless, no *single justified behavior repair* addresses the global cause. Immediate MOVE/BUY/EAT effects are intentional; suppressing their ordinary next-tick decisions could prevent the needed meal sequence, while adding fake duration is forbidden by the frozen semantics. A rule-feasibility filter on prior names might omit the three infeasible hints; the model's replacement decisions were not observed, E is primary in **0/7** cells, and there is no evidence that filtering would resolve the dominant five-cell chain. The engineering decision is therefore `BEHAVIORAL_CAUSE_UNRESOLVED`, with **runtime behavior change = NONE** and `runtime_guard=NONE`. The architecture repair is harness-only. Since no retrieval, trigger or decision behavior changed, the optional 8-episode/32-request **Stage C3 confirmation** is **NOT_NEEDED** and no real provider was called in this closure.

Question 1 is **CLOSED_ENGINEERING at the independent decision layer** (current smallest passing tested corpus BTRAIN_ALL=2,572, prior R1), but **PARTIALLY_UNRESOLVED at the continuous-behavior layer**. The complete cause inventory remains: Core5 action-space incompleteness; 90-minute legacy WORK duration versus empirical ~270; rejected/repeated proposals; missing local behavior cue; here-observed immediate-action/decision-cap churn; and provider truncation, which must never be counted as idle. The reference context recommendation is compact state + needs/obligation + **1–3 short recent events** + R1, not a jointly tested 1–3-event range. Last relevant event is a minimum *candidate*; `C3_recent3` is the actually tested runtime event policy, distinct from the optional eight-episode Stage C3 confirmation. This stage did not perform a new history ablation.

Question 2 remains **CLOSED_ENGINEERING**: money, inventory, stock, location validity, seller presence, state consistency, activity conflict and world consequences are deterministic; open human diaries calibrate ontology, comparable durations, time-of-day/transition distributions and behavior support; the model proposes the next action. `OPEN_DATA_CALIBRATION_WORTHWHILE=YES`: Core5/Core7 all-adult minute coverage 82.17%/93.00%, employed-weekday Core7 94.61%, WORK 90→~270 minute calibration, and the auditable time/transition retrieval index all support that boundary. Open data does not force an individual's schedule.

**Final status: RESEARCH QUESTION 1 PARTIALLY UNRESOLVED.** This is a narrow continuity limitation, not a reversal of Stage A's decision-level result or Question 2's ownership conclusion. The experimental [recommended reference profile](../config/experimental/neutral_day_recommended_reference_v1.yaml) adds this closure's provenance and `runtime_guard=NONE`; it does not alter production defaults. Local 8B, new ontology, new corpus sweep, new context ablation and full-day provider work were not started.
